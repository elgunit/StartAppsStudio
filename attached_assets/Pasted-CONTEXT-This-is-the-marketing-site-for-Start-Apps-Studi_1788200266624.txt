COPY

Heading: One build. Web, iOS, and Android.

Lead: Most studios treat mobile as a second project. New codebase, new
budget, new timeline, and a version of your product that drifts out of
sync with the web one by month three. That is not how these ship. Your
product is built once. When it needs to live on a phone, the same build
goes to the App Store and Google Play as a real native binary with real
device access.

Subhead 1: What that actually means
Body 1: Face ID and Touch ID sign in. Push notifications that reach
people who are not looking at their inbox. Background location. Native
camera and photo library. In app purchases billed through Apple and
Google, so you keep the conversion rate that comes with a payment sheet
people already trust. Encrypted storage that survives a reinstall.
Offline media that works on a plane. Not a bookmark with an icon. A
signed binary in both stores.

Subhead 2: You own the whole thing
Body 2: Native project files are handed over with the rest of your
code. Xcode and Android Studio, full source, in your account. Most app
builders keep the native layer locked in a platform you rent. If you
leave, you leave with a web app and start mobile over. Here, if you
grow past this studio, your next team opens the projects and keeps
building. The exit is designed in from the first commit.

Subhead 3: And it does not double the bill
Body 3: One codebase means one thing to design, one thing to test, one
thing to maintain. Store submission, code signing, and provisioning are
handled. Updates reach your users the day you ship them instead of the
week review gets to them.

Closing line, styled smaller and muted, centred below the three
columns: Store delivery is available on Prototype and above.
